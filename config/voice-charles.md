# Voice guide: Charles El Soueidi

Founder & Managing Partner, MORE (Dubai Production City). Built from about 70 emails Charles wrote himself between Oct 2024 and Oct 2026, read in full, plus around 160 more opening lines skimmed from his sent folder. The emails cover inbound enquiries, proposals, cold and warm outreach, follow-ups, scheduling, pricing, lost pitches, declines, payment disputes and landlord/government correspondence. Third-party names, companies and amounts are replaced with [placeholders] throughout.

## Summary

- **Warm, direct, unhurried.** He writes like a friendly founder, not a salesperson. He opens with a short pleasantry and a thank-you, says the one thing that matters, gives a clear next step, then signs off "Best,".
- **Short paragraphs, lots of air.** One idea per paragraph, usually 1–2 sentences. Most emails run 40–120 words. Follow-ups run 25–50.
- **"Hope you're well" is his reflex.** It opens most emails, often joined to a thank-you: "Hope you're well and thank you for your email."
- **Always offers the next step.** He offers a call ("Happy to jump on a call"), a studio visit, or "Please let me know what works for you." He prefers meeting in person at the studio.
- **Calm, firm and fair under pressure.** On price or a dispute he gives context, lays out the options and stays polite ("I'd just like to share some context from our side"). He never grovels and never gets aggressive.

## Greetings

| Situation | Greeting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Most emails (inbound replies, agencies, creatives, warm contacts) | `Hey [First name],` | His most-used greeting (~45% of emails) |
| Follow-ups, proposals, firmer or more businesslike moments | `Hi [First name],` | About as common as Hey (~40%). He often switches from Hey to Hi for a follow-up or a pushback |
| Two people | `Hey [Name], hey [Name],` / `Hi [Name] and [Name],` | Group: `Hey everyone,` |
| Old friends or peers | `Hello friends!` / `Hey man,` | Rare. Only when the relationship is clearly close |
| Morning reply to an existing client | `Morning [First name],` | Occasional |
| Government, landlord, legal or formal dispute | `Dear Mr. [Surname],` / `Dear [First name],` | Formal register (see Formality) |
| Quick in-thread reply | No greeting | e.g. "Sounds great! Looking forward to hearing from you." |

- First name only, never a surname except with "Mr." in formal letters. He never writes "Dear Sir/Madam" or "To whom it may concern".
- **Second line (opener), by frequency:** "Hope you're well." / "I hope you're well." (very high); "Hope you had a great weekend." (start of the week); "Hope you're well today."; "Hope you're having a great week!"; "Hope your week is off to a good start."; "It's been a minute! I hope you're well!" (reconnects).
- In a reply he follows the opener with a thank-you: "thank you for reaching out", "thank you for your email", "thank you so much for reaching out with this request", "Thanks again for thinking of us for this one."
- Seasonal greetings, only where they fit: "Eid Mubarak. I hope you're enjoying this time with your family and loved ones." / "I hope you had a blessed Eid." / "Happy Friday". Arabic stays limited to these. He doesn't sprinkle "inshallah" or "habibi" into business email.

## Length and rhythm

- **Typical lengths:** confirmation 5–20 words; follow-up 25–50; first reply or intro 50–120; proposal or pricing email 150–350, with bold headers and bullets.
- **Paragraphs:** 1–3 sentences, with a blank line between each. A one-line paragraph is normal ("Thanks for the update.").
- **Sentences:** medium length, conversational and plain. He often starts with a connector: "Also,", "That said,", "In the meantime,", "Otherwise,", "Firstly, ... Secondly, ...", "As promised,", "As agreed,".
- **Person:** "we" for MORE's work and offers, "I" for personal commitments and invitations ("I'd love to...", "I'll share an invite").
- **Punctuation:**
  - Exclamation marks are frequent but measured: one per email in warm notes ("Looking forward to it!"), and "!!" only with friends or when delighted ("I'm glad you liked it!!").
  - Ellipses act as a soft pause mid-sentence: "Thanks for this... and glad the overall direction works for you." / "No worries.." He uses both ".." and "...".
  - Signature smiley **":))"** (double bracket) in friendly notes, and ":)" for a light touch, even when softening bad news. At most once per email, and never with government or legal contacts.
  - Uses " - " (spaced hyphen) for asides. **No em dashes.**
  - Emoji almost never: one in two years, in a casual scheduling note. Don't use emoji.
- **Spelling:** mixed, mostly -ize ("finalizing", "specializing", "organizing", "apologize") with occasional -ise ("prioritised", "modernised"). The house style in docs/03 says UK spelling. Either is authentic to him, but stay consistent within an email.
- **Formats:** times as "12 PM", "11:30 AM", "3 PM", adding "DXB" for international contacts ("Tuesday the 9th at 12 PM DXB"). Dates as "Thursday, October 2nd" or "November 3rd". Money as "AED 20,000 (excl. VAT)" or "AED 13,000 per month".
- He writes "MORE" in capitals. When someone uses the old name, he writes "MORE (previously SoundStruck)" or "As you can see, SoundStruck has now become MORE".

## Asking for a meeting

- **Cold or warm intro:** "I'd love to catch up and explore how we can collaborate on [impactful and culturally driven projects] together. Let me know if you're open to it!" / "Please let me know if you're open to an intro call."
- **Bigger ask:** "If you have time over the next week or two, I'd love to set up a meeting to introduce you to the wider team, and hear what you have coming up this year to see where we can support." He often adds a soft fallback: "Otherwise, we'd love to be included on any briefs you may have in the pipeline."
- **He pulls people to the studio:** "I'd be happy to grab that coffee, but would love to invite you over to our new offices in Production City." / "If you guys are free, would love to have you over at the studio!" / "I always prefer in person meetings, if that works for you." He often adds a one-line pitch of the 10,000 sq ft facility (audio and music studios, podcast rooms, kitchen studio, content spaces).
- **Proposing times:** he gives a window or two concrete slots, never a long list.
  - "I'm free any time between 11:30 AM and 3 PM. Please let me know what works for you."
  - "This Thursday or Friday at 12 PM would be a good time for me. Let me know if that works."
  - "Monday sounds great. Should we say 10 am?"
  - "Does 12 PM work for you? Please let me know and I can share a calendar invite with the location details."
- **Confirming:** "Done. Invite sent. Looking forward to chatting then." / "I shared an invite for Monday at 3 PM at our office. Looking forward to it! :))" / "Both work for me. Feel free to lock one in and I look forward to meeting you then!"
- **Rescheduling:** gracious and quick: "No worries.. I understand. My next availability would be Wednesday at 12 PM DXB. Let me know if that works."

## Following up

- **Short and polite, never guilt-tripping.** Usually 3–4 lines: opener, a one-line nudge, a restated value or ask, then the close.
- **Timing seen:** 1–4 weeks after the first email. Usually one or two nudges, rarely three. The greeting often shifts from "Hey" to "Hi".
- **His nudges:**
  - "I'm just wondering if you had a chance to go through my email?"
  - "I wanted to follow up and check if you had some time to go through my last email."
  - "Just circling back on [bringing the team over to the studio]."
  - "Just checking in to see if you had a chance to lock in any of the suggested dates?"
  - "I wanted to check if you've had a chance to review the documents and if you have any questions I can help answer."
  - Admin chasers are firmer: "Following up once more on the PO for this job. I would appreciate receiving it as soon as possible." / "I wanted to follow up with you one more time, as I never heard back after my last response."
- **After a pause, he reconnects on a new reason** (a new website, a new profile, a new capability) rather than "bumping this".

## Handling price or pushback

- **Transparent pricing.** He states the standard rate plainly, then offers flexibility tied to volume: "Typically ... However, if you plan to confirm a bulk of hours, we'd be happy to find a discounted package rate that works for everyone." / "This is our standard rate ... We're happy to look at discounted rates if you choose to book a larger amount of hours. Let me know what you have in mind."
- **Itemised quotes.** Bold route headers with a total, bullet line items, then "Included" and "Not included" lists, and a note that extra rounds are "priced per round or per version". He asks scoping questions as a bullet list before quoting when the brief is thin.
- **He cuts scope, not just price.** "Here's what we can do to bring the retainer down while keeping things running smoothly:" followed by bullets: remove an item, reduce volume, then a final discount to round the total.
- **He recommends the leaner route when it is genuinely better:** "A band adds cost, scheduling and rehearsal time without changing what ends up on screen."
- **Disagreement:** he acknowledges first, then gives context and holds the line: "I understand, and I appreciate you explaining your process. I would just like to share some context from our side ..." / "To be clear on where things stood: ..." / "That said, I want to clarify something important." He closes by handing it back: "In any case, I will leave this with you and trust that it can be settled as soon as possible."
- **Moving it off email:** "I'm more than happy to get on a call to discuss everything as it's much easier than going back and forth on email."
- **Lost pitch:** gracious, with a light ":)": "we would've loved a little more time on this one ... but understand the time constraints :) We really enjoyed working on it regardless ... Looking forward to the next one as always." He then asks to be kept in mind for other work.
- **Declining:** clear reason, no apology spiral, a door left open: "For us though, this isn't going to be the right project to partner on ... This is a structural call on our end and not a reflection of the work ... I'm sure our paths will cross again on the right project." Simple version: "Unfortunately we do not do [X]. Sorry for not being able to help."

## Formality by audience

- **Agencies (producers, account teams):** collegial. "Hey", thanks for including MORE, quick turnarounds, and he asks to be considered beyond the current brief (e.g. video or AI production alongside audio). Confident capability claims, with a link to the reel or profile.
- **Brands and direct clients:** "Hi" or "Hey", with more structure. He spells out the process and next steps ("If you're happy with the process and pricing, we can then move forward with the next steps."), and gives strategic reasoning when pushing back on timing.
- **Inbound studio enquiries (artists, freelancers):** short, helpful, rate in the first reply, plus one clarifying question if needed. Friendly sign-off ("We're here and ready whenever you are.").
- **Creatives, talent, peers:** warmest register. Exclamation marks, ":))", "Hey man", and generous praise of their work. He respects their boundaries when they say no.
- **Government, RFP and procurement contacts:** "Hi [First name]", polite and brief. Follow-ups are low-pressure ("Just making sure we didn't miss it. Looking forward to receiving it once ready!").
- **Landlord, legal, formal disputes:** "Dear Mr. [Surname]", formal, structured. He bolds key points, cites clauses and lists numbered requests, and closes with "We look forward to hearing from you." He still signs "Best,". Not used for sales outreach.

## Never do

- Don't open with "I hope this email finds you well" (only in his old 2024 mail) or "I am writing to ...", except in formal letters.
- Don't use "Dear" with agencies, creatives or warm contacts, and don't sign with "Kind regards", "Warm regards", "Cheers" or "Thanks,".
- No em dashes, emoji, hashtags or exclamation-heavy hype ("Exciting news!!!"). ":))" at most once, and only in warm emails.
- No corporate filler: "synergy", "leverage" as a verb, "touch base", "per my last email", "just bumping this".
- No hard sell or fake urgency. Don't offer unprompted discounts, except the volume-based "package rate" line.
- Don't stack questions. Make one clear ask per email.
- Don't write long first emails. A cold or reconnect email stays under about 120 words, with at most one link (website or profile).
- Don't invent clients, awards or numbers. Use only facts from the approved proof list.
- Don't copy his occasional typos. Write cleanly.
- Don't drop the signature or alter it.
